Privacy Policy

Draft. This policy describes what Beach Peek does today, and each statement was checked against the app itself. It has not yet been reviewed by a lawyer, and it will be before Beach Peek is offered in any app store.

Last updated: 13 October 2026

Beach Peek tells you what the conditions are at Santa Barbara County beaches. This policy explains, in plain language, what the app keeps and what leaves your phone.

The short version: Beach Peek has no account, no ads and no tracking. Your choices stay on your phone. Your location, if you turn it on, is used on your phone and is never sent to us or anyone else.

What stays on your phone

Everything you tell the app:

  • your go-to beaches, their order, and which one opens first
  • your default activity, surf level, and °F/°C choice
  • your morning-reminder days and time
  • whether you turned location on
  • the fact that you accepted the safety notice (which version, when, and the app version)
  • the last conditions the app downloaded, so it can show them when you are offline

This is stored only in the app's own storage on your device. We cannot see it. Deleting the app deletes it.

Your location

Location is optional and off until you allow it.

If you allow it, the app reads your position while the app is open and uses it, on your phone, for one thing: the distance to each beach.

Your position is not saved, not sent to us, and not sent to any weather or map service. You can turn it off in Settings, or in your phone's own settings, at any time.

What leaves your phone

Beach Peek does not send anything you entered. Like any app that shows live information, it does make two kinds of download, and each one reaches a company that hosts the files:

  1. Conditions. The app downloads a small public file for each beach from our site, which is hosted by GitHub. As with any web request, GitHub can see your internet (IP) address and basic technical details of the request. We add no name, account, location or identifier to it.
  2. App updates. The app checks for updates through Expo, the service we build the app with. That check includes the app version, whether the phone is Android or iPhone, and a random install number the update system creates so it can count installs. It is not tied to your name, and Expo can see your IP address when the check is made.

The conditions themselves come from public sources — Santa Barbara County, NOAA, the National Weather Service and Open-Meteo. Our server fetches those, not your phone, so those organizations never receive a request from you through the app.

If you tap a link in the app (for example, the county's status map), it opens in your browser, and that website's own privacy policy applies.

What Beach Peek does NOT do

  • No account. There is nothing to sign in to.
  • No ads, and no advertising identifiers.
  • No analytics or tracking. We do not measure how you use the app.
  • We never sell or share your personal information, with anyone, for any purpose.
  • No contacts, photos, camera, microphone or calendar access.
  • No location tracking in the background. Location is read only while the app is open, and only if you allowed it.

Notifications

If you turn on the morning reminder and allow notifications, the reminder is scheduled on your device by Android or iOS. Nothing is sent to a server to make it happen, we cannot see it, and it does not contain your location.

Children

Beach Peek is a planning tool for adults, including parents. It is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone.

California residents

We do not sell or share personal information, and we do not collect it in the first place. If you have a question about your rights under California law, contact us at the address below.

Keeping and deleting data

Everything the app keeps is on your phone: delete the app to delete it. We hold nothing about you, so there is nothing for us to delete. Standard request logs kept by GitHub and Expo are governed by their own policies.

Changes to this policy

If what the app does with information changes — for example, if a future version offers alerts that need a server — this policy will be updated first, and the "last updated" date above will change.
